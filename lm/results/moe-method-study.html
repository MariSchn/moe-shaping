MoE Router Method Study

Nine ways to train (or not train) the router of the same mixture-of-experts language model, compared on validation perplexity, load balance (MaxVio) and expert specialization. Unbounded LOLA opponent shaping diverged at this scale; a capped variant is included.

What the sweep shows

    Main metric

    Validation perplexity

    Difference in final validation loss from the no-load-balancing baseline, in nats (lower is better). Each dot is one seed, the bar is the mean, and the grey band spans the two baseline seeds. Perplexity is exp(loss) on 10.5M held-out tokens after 1,500 steps.

    Load balance

    MaxVio over training

    MaxViobatch = (maxi loadi − mean load) / mean load on each global batch of 1M tokens, averaged over the 12 MoE layers (Wang et al., 2024). 0 is perfect balance; 7 means one expert receives every token (its ceiling with 64 experts and top-8). Seed-averaged, with the baseline in grey on every panel.

    Expert collapse

    How much of the expert capacity is used

    Measured on the 2.6M-token domain set after training. Dead experts receive less than 10% of their uniform share, counted over all 12 × 64 = 768 experts. Effective experts is exp(entropy of the load) per layer, with 64 as the maximum. MaxVioglobal is computed over the full validation set.

    Specialization (OLMoE metrics)

    How specialized the experts are

    From OLMoE (Muennighoff et al., 2024), on a held-out set of five domains: Wikipedia, GitHub, arXiv, books and C4 web text, 128 × 4,096 tokens each. Domain NMI is the mutual information between a token's domain and its experts, normalized by the domain entropy (0 = routing ignores domain). Vocabulary specialization: per expert, the average share of a routed token type's occurrences that go to that expert (uniform = k/E = 0.125). Co-activation: for each expert, the highest fraction of its tokens that also go to one particular other expert (uniform ≈ 0.11). Lower co-activation means less redundancy.

    Domain specialization in the last layer

    The fraction of each domain's tokens routed to each expert, as a multiple of the uniform share. Experts are sorted by the domain they favour most. Seed 42.

    Router saturation and per-domain perplexity

    Saturation: the share of each token's top-8 experts at a given point in training that are still among its top-8 at the end (OLMoE's definition, averaged over layers on a fixed probe set). Higher early saturation means routing settled sooner.
