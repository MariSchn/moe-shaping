moe-shaping · 1.3B-parameter MoE · 64 experts, top-8 · 1.57B tokens

MoE Router Method Study

Nine ways to train (or not train) the router of the same mixture-of-experts language model, compared on validation perplexity, load balance (MaxVio) and expert specialization. Unbounded LOLA opponent shaping diverged at this scale; a capped variant is included.

Code audit, 23 Sep · read before the results

Three method arms are affected by implementation bugs

An independent audit of the exact code that ran found bugs that invalidate some conclusions below. The load-balancing comparison is unaffected: baseline, aux-loss, aux-free, sigmoid control, their sweeps and all balance/specialization metrics were verified correct. Affected:

  • Bandit and bandit + gradient: the REINFORCE gradient also flows through the router input into the whole backbone (at the final checkpoint 0.8–1.3× the task gradient in the lowest layers). The measured deficit reflects that leak, not bandit routing as a method. The routing code itself is correct: bitwise identical to standard routing with REINFORCE and exploration off.

  • LOLA: the same leak. The correction reaches the backbone at 1.1–1.5× the task gradient, partly opposing it. The divergences and the 11.9 perplexity cannot be attributed to opponent shaping. The closed form itself is verified correct.

  • Frozen random router: not actually frozen. It trained at a small, rising learning rate from the schedule's minimum LR, and seed 42 additionally resumed with a corrupted optimizer group mapping. Treat 8.57 as approximate.

  • Bilevel (design, not a bug): the experts, ~93% of parameters, are frozen during router phases, so its cost measures withheld expert updates rather than routing.

Fixes and re-runs for these arms are pending. Full audit: /iopsstor/scratch/cscs/smarian/moe-shaping/audit/REPORT.md.

What the sweep shows

    Main metric

    Validation perplexity

    Difference in final validation loss from the no-load-balancing baseline, in nats (lower is better). Each dot is one seed, the bar is the mean, and the grey band spans the two baseline seeds. Perplexity is exp(loss) on 10.5M held-out tokens after 1,500 steps.

    Load balance

    MaxVio over training

    MaxViobatch = (maxi loadi − mean load) / mean load on each global batch of 1M tokens, averaged over the 12 MoE layers (Wang et al., 2024). 0 is perfect balance; 7 means one expert receives every token (its ceiling with 64 experts and top-8). Seed-averaged, with the baseline in grey on every panel.

    Expert collapse

    How much of the expert capacity is used

    Measured on the 2.6M-token domain set after training. Dead experts receive less than 10% of their uniform share, counted over all 12 × 64 = 768 experts. Effective experts is exp(entropy of the load) per layer, with 64 as the maximum. MaxVioglobal is computed over the full validation set.

    Specialization (OLMoE metrics)

    How specialized the experts are

    From OLMoE (Muennighoff et al., 2024). The model trains only on ClimbMix, whose Megatron copy has no domain labels, so these metrics use a separate held-out set built for the analysis from five public Hugging Face datasets, the first documents in stream order, each capped at 16k tokens and cut into 128 × 4,096 tokens per domain: Wikipedia (wikimedia/wikipedia, 78 documents), GitHub (codeparrot/codeparrot-clean-valid, Python only, 166), arXiv (ccdv/arxiv-summarization article bodies, 68), books (emozilla/pg19-test, 34) and web (allenai/c4 validation, 1,057). Caveats: that arXiv source is pre-processed (lowercased, spaces around punctuation), unlike real arXiv or anything in ClimbMix; books and arXiv rest on few documents; and ClimbMix is web-derived, so the C4 and Wikipedia samples may overlap its training data. Domain NMI is the mutual information between a token's domain and its experts, normalized by the domain entropy (0 = routing ignores domain). Vocabulary specialization: per expert, the average share of a routed token type's occurrences that go to that expert (uniform = k/E = 0.125). Co-activation: for each expert, the highest fraction of its tokens that also go to one particular other expert (uniform ≈ 0.11). Lower co-activation means less redundancy.

    Domain specialization in the last layer

    The fraction of each domain's tokens routed to each expert, as a multiple of the uniform share. Experts are sorted by the domain they favour most; a block that is dark for one domain only is a group of experts serving mainly that domain. A column dark in every domain is an overloaded expert, which is load imbalance rather than specialization (this metric mixes the two), and an empty column is a dead expert. All methods, seed 42, ordered by perplexity. Note that the bandit, LOLA and frozen rows come from the runs the audit found to be affected by bugs.

    Router saturation and per-domain perplexity

    Saturation: the share of each token's top-8 experts at a given point in training that are still among its top-8 at the end (OLMoE's definition, averaged over layers on a fixed probe set). Higher early saturation means routing settled sooner.
